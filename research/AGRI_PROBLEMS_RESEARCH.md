## Main-session findings (direct research)

### P1. Pesticide spray labor health exposure — VERIFIED ✅
**Evidence:** Yavatmal, Maharashtra (Jul–Nov 2017, cotton belt): 50+ deaths across Vidarbha (20+ in Yavatmal district alone), ~800–886 hospitalizations for acute poisoning from spray inhalation/contact; several hundred cases of temporary blindness. Root causes: knapsack spraying without protective gear, 8–10 hr shifts, taller Bt cotton bringing nozzles to face height, wrong pesticide cocktails. State compensation ₹2 lakh per death.
**Sources:** [Public Eye investigation](https://toxicexports.publiceye.ch/), [PAN India report (PDF)](https://pan-india.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/Yavatmal-Report_PAN-India_Oct-2017_web.pdf), [Down to Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/yavatmal-farmers-fight-losing-battle-against-pesticide-poisoning-pin-hopes-on-swiss-court-hearing-92490), [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/india-18-maharashtra-farmers-die-of-pesticide-poisoning-probe-ordered-1.2100089)
**Root cause:** humans carry the sprayer; exposure is inherent to manual blanket spraying.
**Verdict: PARTIAL→SOLVABLE (strong).** Rover carries the nozzle instead of a human. Even in v1 (small tank, mist nozzles), removing the human from drift range for targeted spot applications directly attacks the exposure pathway. Full replacement of high-volume canopy spraying needs a bigger platform (PARTIAL) — but "no human behind the nozzle" is the pitch line, and Yavatmal is the evidence.

### P2. Post-harvest losses (national baseline) — VERIFIED ✅ (strengthened 03-Aug from primary docs)
**Evidence:** ICAR-CIPHET 2015 national study: ₹92,651 crore/year quantitative losses across 45 commodities; fruits ₹16,644 cr, vegetables ₹14,842 cr; per-crop losses up to ~16% (guava highest). NABCONS 2022 update: fruits 6.02–15.05%, vegetables 4.87–11.61%; horticulture losses ~49.9 million tonnes/yr, total food loss value ~₹1.53 lakh crore; inadequate cold chain named primary driver. Sorting/grading stage alone = 3.26% loss (Jha et al. 2015).
**Primary-source confirmation (read directly, copies in `.research/`):** MoFPI's official Rajya Sabha answer (USQ 572, 07-Feb-2025) tabulates the NABCONS 2022 study for 2020-21: cereals 12.49 MMT lost (3.89–5.92%), fruits 7.36 MMT (6.02–15.05%), vegetables 11.97 MMT (4.87–11.61%). From the NABCONS report itself, crop-level national PHL: **grapes 7.15%** (farm ops 5.09% + market 2.05%; biggest farm contributors = sorting/grading 2.43% and harvesting 1.53%; Nashik was the LOWEST-loss district at 6.69% thanks to APEDA's 160 Maharashtra pack houses), **onion 7.26%** (harvesting 2.48% + sorting/grading 1.01% dominate; Ahmednagar lowest at 6.71%), **mango 8.53%** (harvesting 1.80% + sorting/grading 2.74% dominate). Improper/careless harvesting is repeatedly named the top farm-level loss driver.
**Study credentials (for the pitch):** NABCONS study = 54 commodities, 292 districts, 15 agro-climatic zones, reference year 2020–22, commissioned by MoFPI — confirmed by three PIB releases. Literature cross-check: Sharma et al. 2018 review put Indian grape PHL at **8.23–16%** across studies (NABCONS's 7.15% is the low, recent end); Murthy et al. 2014 (AP, Thompson Seedless): 7.82% field + 12.13% cold-storage losses for the export chain. Pomegranate (the grape belt's companion crop): Dhinesh & Ramasamy 2016 review put Indian pomegranate PHL at **20–40%** — the worst of the major fruits; tomato national PHL **11.61%** (see P8).
**Sources:** [Down to Earth (Parliament, Aug 2024)](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/as-told-to-parliament-august-6-2024-4-8-grains-5-15-fruits-vegetables-lost-after-harvest), [WRI India Food Loss & Waste report (PDF)](https://wri-india.org/sites/default/files/Food%20Losss%20and%20Waste_August%202021.pdf), [tomato supply chain study (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9170466/), MoFPI RS USQ 572 (`.research/mofpi_572.pdf`), NABCONS full report (`.research/nabcons_phl.pdf`), [PIB 1885038](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1885038), [PIB 2148529](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2148529), [PIB 2151371](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2151371)
**Root cause:** harvest timing mismatch + no pre-cooling + cold chain gaps + manual/no grading.
**Verdict: PARTIAL.** Rover cannot build cold chain. But the harvest-timing slice (cut-when-ready via ripeness/yield mapping → less storage dwell time) is the rover-addressable share — grapes are the sharpest case (non-climacteric, ~2-day rot window per TIH field engineers).

### P7. Wild animal crop damage (nilgai/boar) — VERIFIED ✅
**Evidence:** Farmers in Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Haryana, MP, Maharashtra, Rajasthan and UP have petitioned to cull nilgai; herds "raid and trample crop fields," sometimes causing food shortages; sugarcane farmers urged to switch to pulses due to damage (ToI 2015). Bihar declared nilgai vermin (allowing hunting); UP granted culling rights; Neemuch (MP) farmers held a 2015 hunger strike demanding compensation. Farmers use live electric wires as guards — which kill other animals (and people).
**Sources:** [Wikipedia: Nilgai (crop damage section)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgai) — citing Goyal & Rajpurohit 2000 (UP J. Zoology), Economic Times 2015 (Bihar vermin), Times of India 2015, The Guardian 2014.
**Root cause:** shrinking habitat + legal protection + no effective non-lethal deterrent at field scale.
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE (honest).** A 10kg rover neither deters a 250kg nilgai nor patrols at night reliably in v1. Camera-based intrusion alerts are a stretch goal at best. Listed to show we know the problem landscape and are honest about scope — this needs fencing/policy, not our rover.

### P8. Boom-bust price crashes / market gluts (tomato as flagship) — VERIFIED ✅
**Evidence:** Recurring cycle, well documented. 2023: prices surged >300% (₹24.68→₹108.92/kg wholesale, ₹200/kg retail July) after floods in AP/Maharashtra/Karnataka → mass replanting → crash to ₹10/kg; Kolar APMC (Asia's 2nd-largest tomato market) received 4.21 lakh quintals in one month vs 2.31 lakh the prior year; a 15kg box fell from ₹2,300 to ₹45–120; many Kolar farmers didn't harvest at all because labour cost > market return. Telangana: 25kg box at ₹80–100 (~₹4/kg), tomatoes dumped on Srisailam Road. Mysuru: ₹120–150→₹5 per crate in a fortnight, dumped in drains. Prior episodes: Dec 2016 Chhattisgarh 25 paise/kg, 45,000 kg dumped on a highway; 2020 Haryana ₹3/kg.
**Sources:** [NBC (2023 surge)](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/world/indias-tomato-prices-surge-300-sparking-theft-turmoil-rcna94000), [Deccan Herald (₹200→₹10 crash)](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Frs-200-to-rs-10-tomato-farmers-hopes-crash-2700660), [Tribune (₹4/kg glut)](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/tomato-prices-crash-to-rs-4-kg-amid-supply-glut-in-most-growing-states-304213), [IndToday (Telangana dumping)](https://indtoday.com/tomato-price-crash-farmers-dump-crop-on-road/)
**NABCONS 2022 tomato numbers (added 03-Aug from primary doc):** national tomato PHL **11.61%** (farm 8.37% + market 3.25%); biggest single loss operation = sorting/grading (3.1%); improper hand-plucking named the harvesting loss driver. Sharp twist for the pitch: **Kolar had the LOWEST district losses (9.46%)** of all surveyed — the 2023 Kolar collapse was a pure market/glut failure, not a handling failure. Post-harvest handling can be good and the farmer still loses everything to synchronized oversupply — which is exactly the yield-visibility gap.
**Root cause:** no yield visibility → everyone plants the same crop after a price spike → synchronized glut; no cold storage to buffer; perishables can't wait.
**Verdict: PARTIAL.** Rover cannot fix markets. But the *yield-forecast* slice is real: bunch/fruit counting + maturity mapping gives a farmer early, quantified yield estimates → stagger harvest, pre-sell, or divert to processing before the crash. Same computer-vision stack as the grape wedge. Honest framing: helps one farmer time/plan; does not solve the systemic glut.

### P9. Farm labour shortage & wage spiral — VERIFIED ✅
**Evidence:** NSSO: 34 million workers LEFT agriculture between 2004-05 and 2011-12 — first-ever decline; agriculture still ~46% of workforce (PLFS 2023-24: 46.1%) and 64% of rural employment. Karnataka study: wages rose ₹60→₹110/day post-MGNREGS; 91.67% of farmers hire outside labour at peak paying "exorbitant" rates; labour scarcity shifts harvest timing off the agronomic optimum (e.g., wheat harvest moved off 2nd week of April). Kolar 2023: harvesting abandoned because labour cost exceeded returns. Non-farm work (electrician, mason) outbids field wages.
**Sources:** [Down to Earth "Labour exit"](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/labour-exit-51974), [Current Agriculture Research Journal (Karnataka coping study)](http://www.agriculturejournal.org/volume6number2/farmers-strategies-to-cope-labour-shortage-in-northern-and-southern-dry-zones-of-karnataka-india/), [TPCI/India Business & Trade (Prof. Brajesh Jha)](https://www.tpci.in/indiabusinesstrade/blogs/labour-shortage-in-indian-agriculture/), [Labour Scarcity in Agriculture: A Review (2022)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361253707_Labour_Scarcity_in_Agriculture_A_Review)
**Root cause:** migration + MGNREGS floor + better non-farm wages; scouting/spraying/monitoring are labour-intensive tasks competing for scarce hands.
**Verdict: SOLVABLE (as labour substitution for scouting & spot-spray).** The rover's core function — walking every row, looking at every plant, spraying only where needed — is exactly the labour that is disappearing. Quantifiable pitch: one scouting pass over 1 acre ≈ hours of human walking; ₹110+/day/person at peak. NOT solvable: harvesting labour (no arm — rover only *plans* the harvest so hired labour-days are used efficiently).

### P10. Unseasonal rain / hail destroying ready-to-harvest grapes (Nashik) — VERIFIED ✅
**Evidence:** 2025 (October rains): ~45,000 hectares of Nashik vineyards severely damaged (of 60,000 ha perennial fruit affected); estimated loss **₹3,500 crore**; two grape growers died by suicide amid the losses; consultancy Qualicrop estimated ~50% average crop loss for the season — white seedless varieties 40–70% loss, black seedless 30–50%. Grower quote: "The October rains of 2025 not only delayed the crop, they destroyed it — a systemic failure of the crop cycle." Prior: Nov 26, 2023 hailstorm blanketed ready-to-harvest vineyards (crores in damage; farmers with crop covers were spared); earlier Maharashtra-wide event damaged 34,000 ha (onion, grapes, pomegranate). Vulnerability window: rain during berry development → rot; rain at harvest → total loss.
**Sources:** [Free Press Journal (₹3,500 cr, 45,000 ha)](https://www.freepressjournal.in/pune/nashik-grapes-hit-hard-by-rains-45000-hectares-of-vineyards-damaged-3500-crore-loss-feared), [Fruitnet/Qualicrop damage assessment](https://www.fruitnet.com/asiafruit/qualicrop-assesses-rain-damage-to-indian-grapes/269574.article), [Mongabay India (crop covers)](https://india.mongabay.com/2024/03/nashiks-grape-farmers-shield-crops-to-save-from-weather-fury/), [Deccan Herald (34,000 ha)](https://www.deccanherald.com/national/west/crops-in-34000-hectare-damaged-because-of-unseasonal-rain-hailstorms-in-maharashtra-1208482.html)
**Root cause:** climate volatility + everything ripening/hanging at once with no prioritized harvest plan; crop covers exist but cost money and are not universal.
**Verdict: PARTIAL (small but real slice).** Rover cannot stop rain — crop covers and insurance are the real fixes. The rover-addressable slice: a GPS ripeness map + yield count means that when a rain forecast gives 48–72h warning, the farmer knows exactly WHICH rows are ripe enough to cut first — salvage-priority harvesting instead of panic harvesting. Strengthens the harvest-intelligence wedge; do not oversell.

### P11. Onion boom-bust + below-cost sales (Lasalgaon) — VERIFIED ✅
**Evidence:** Maharashtra = 35–40% of India's onion production; Lasalgaon = Asia's largest onion market. 2026: average ₹1,000–1,100/quintal vs production cost ~₹1,800/quintal (Maharashtra State Onion Growers Association) — farmers losing ₹600–700/quintal; daily arrivals 40,000–50,000 quintals; ThePrint documented prices as low as ₹1/kg for small varieties; auctions halted by protests, 8-day ultimatum, earlier riots at Lasalgaon/Pimpalgaon/Yeola (produce dumped, buses stoned). Nov 2022: ₹10–15/kg vs cultivation cost ₹22–25/kg. One farmer: December rains destroyed ~50% of his crop and delayed/damaged quality. Export demand collapse (Bangladesh absent a year) worsens gluts.
**Sources:** [ThePrint (₹1/kg)](https://theprint.in/india/rs-1-per-kg-maharashtras-red-gold-turns-farmers-lives-to-ash-why-onion-prices-have-crashed/2931111/), [Down to Earth (protests)](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/onion-price-crash-maharashtra-farmers-to-protest-next-week-unless-government-increases-costs-86214), [Tribune (riots)](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/nation/onion-farmers-run-riot-as-price-crashes-49947), [FreshPlaza (supply surge)](https://www.freshplaza.com/asia/article/9810511/maharashtra-onion-prices-fall-to-us-12-per-100-kg-following-supply-surge/)
**Root cause:** synchronized planting + storage rot forcing distress sales + demand shocks. (Kanda chawl 30–40% storage-loss figure still ⚠️ unverified — P4.)
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE (mostly).** Storage structures and market policy are not rover problems. Thin PARTIAL slice: pre-storage screening (only sound bulbs into storage) and yield forecasts — but honest answer for Baramati: onions are not our wedge; grapes are.

### P4. Onion storage rot in kanda chawl — VERIFIED ✅ (03-Aug, from primary documents in `.research/`)
**Evidence:** Traditional kanda chawl storage losses are even worse than the provisional 30–40% claim. PoCRA/IIT-B kanda chaal feasibility study (field surveys + interviews + literature): traditional structures lose **35% to over 60%** over the storage season — 35% is the *minimum*, achieved only when rain timing, weather, seed variety and harvesting practice are all favorable; IIT-B's experimental CA structure cut losses to 10–12%. NABARD onion-storage model scheme: losses **30–40%** typical; **50–90% recorded over six months** depending on genotype/conditions; loss composition = physiological weight loss 30–40%, rotting 20–30%, sprouting 20–40%; even improved ventilated structures lose ~20–30%/season. ICAR: bottom-and-side-ventilated structures still lose up to **46% in 4 months**; ICAR-DOGR cold storage trial cut total loss to 7.14% vs **54%** in an uncontrolled ventilated structure.
**Sources:** [ICAR onion cold-storage page](https://icar.org.in/en/node/4701), NABARD onion storage scheme (`.research/nabard_onion.pdf`), PoCRA kanda chaal feasibility report (`.research/pocra_kandachaal.pdf`)
**Root cause:** storage structure physics (temperature/humidity/ventilation) + damaged or infected bulbs entering storage (careless harvest → bruising → rot; NABCONS names harvesting as onion's biggest farm-level loss operation at 2.48%).
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE (core), thin PARTIAL slice.** Storage structures are not a rover problem — say so plainly at Baramati. The defensible slice: rot starts with damaged/infected bulbs going INTO storage, so pre-storage screening (camera grading of lots) and in-field disease pressure maps have some value — but the honest pitch is grapes, not onions.

### P13. Mango post-harvest / grading losses — VERIFIED ✅ (03-Aug, NABCONS primary doc)
**Evidence:** NABCONS 2022: mango overall PHL **8.53%** nationally (CIPHET 2015: 9.16%) — farm operations 6.03%, market 2.50%; the two biggest farm-level loss operations are **sorting/grading (2.74%)** and **harvesting (1.80%)**; worst state Bihar 10.10% (improper harvesting + no infrastructure), best AP 7.89% (pack houses, ripening chambers, fruit pickers).
**Sources:** NABCONS PHL report (`.research/nabcons_phl.pdf`, pp. 87–88)
**Root cause:** manual harvest timing guesswork + hand sorting; losses concentrate exactly at harvest+grading, before produce leaves the farm.
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE (mostly) — honest.** The rover doesn't pick or grade mangoes, and orchard canopies are overhead (a ground rover images trunks/low canopy, not crown fruit). Listed because the number proves the harvest+grading pain across fruit crops — the same pain our grape harvest-intelligence wedge attacks where the fruit IS at rover height (trellised vines).

### P12. Farm size: 86% of Indian farmers are small/marginal — VERIFIED ✅ (CONTEXT, not a problem to solve — it DEFINES our price constraint)
**Evidence:** Agriculture Census 2015-16: 86.1% of holdings <2 ha (~126M of 146M holdings); average holding 1.08 ha (down from 2.28 ha in 1970-71); marginal farmers average just 0.38 ha; NABARD: average fell to 0.74 ha by 2021-22 (−31% in 5 years); NSS 77th round (2019): 89.4% of agri households own <2 ha. Small/marginal farms produce ~51% of agri output and ~70% of high-value crops (fruits/vegetables) — i.e., OUR target crops are grown by the smallest farmers. >50% of these farmers live in UP, Bihar, MP, **Maharashtra**, AP.
**Sources:** [PIB (SMF condition)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1910357), [PIB (holdings decrease)](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?relid=199780), [Sambodhi marginal farmer survey (PDF)](https://sambodhi.co.in/sambodhipanels/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/ANNUAL-SURVEY-OF-STATE-OF-MARGINAL-FARMERS-IN-INDIA.pdf), [Arcus Policy Research](https://arcuspolicyresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Arcus-Blog-1_.pdf)
**Why it matters for the pitch:** individual ownership of expensive, unproved machinery is a poor fit for 86% of holdings. The defensible route is an FPO/CHC-operated pay-per-use service with measured farmer and provider economics. Do not attach an ownership or payback claim to the census statistic alone.

### P3. Grape spray burden & spray economics (Nashik) — VERIFIED ✅ (03-Aug via Tavily MCP)
**Evidence:** Crop protection is consistently the **2nd-largest single line item** in grape cost of cultivation across independent Maharashtra farm-economics studies: **16.93%** of cost-C in Sangli (Mhetre et al., IJCMAS 2020, after only rental value of land at 30.16%); **11.51%** of the Nashik orchard establishment cost (Pharma Innovation Journal 2024); **12.4%** for Muscat grapes (Keerthana et al., AJAEES 2021). On spray frequency: ICAR-NRC Grapes / MANAGE "Climate Smart Viticulture" states growers "as a precaution **rigidly follow pre-determined spray schedules** throughout the susceptible period" — i.e., calendar spraying, not need-based. Baseline downy-mildew program = **5–6 sprays in the first 55 days after pruning**, rising to **9 if it rains in Nov–Dec**, falling to 3–4 in a dry spell — plus separate powdery-mildew, anthracnose and pest sprays layered across the full season (the NRC Grapes POP bulletin details a spray at nearly every phenological stage). Nashik grower survey (Extension Journal, 240 growers, 24 villages): **33.3% spray MORE than the recommended number of times, and another 45.84% at the upper recommended limit** — direct evidence of over-application under uncertainty.
**Sources:** [Mhetre et al. 2020, Sangli grape economics (IJCMAS)](https://www.ijcmas.com/special/11/A.V.%20Mhetre,%20et%20al.pdf), [Nashik grape economics (Pharma Innovation 2024)](https://www.thepharmajournal.com/archives/2024/vol13issue2/PartA/13-2-27-982.pdf), [Muscat grape cost analysis (AJAEES 2021)](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/358139/files/Keerthana39112021AJAEES75290.pdf), [MANAGE Climate-Smart Viticulture (spray schedules)](https://www.manage.gov.in/publications/eBooks/Climate%20Smart%20Viticultural%20Technologies.pdf), [ICAR-NRC Grapes disease/pest POP bulletin](https://www.nrcgrapes.in/technical%20bulletins/techbul-8-pop-for-disease-insect-pest-on-grapes.pdf), [Nashik pesticide-use survey (Extension Journal)](https://www.extensionjournal.com/uploads/archives/S-7-11-1-331.pdf)
**Root cause:** disease pressure is real and near-total losses are possible, so growers spray on a fixed calendar "as a precaution" rather than on detected need — driving both cost (2nd-biggest input) and over-application (33% spray above recommendation).
**Verdict: PARTIAL (scouting and records; spray savings unproved).** Early disease evidence can support better-timed advice, treatment records and verification. It does not establish that localized spot treatment can replace whole-canopy protection: [APEDA's current grape procedure](https://apeda.gov.in/sites/default/files/export_procedures/procedureforexportofgrapes_17Feb_2026.pdf) uses a normal high-volume basis of ~1,000 L/ha and requires the recommended active ingredient per hectare for efficacy and residue control. Any fungicide saving must be designed with ICAR-NRC Grapes/qualified agronomists and measured in a controlled trial.

### P5. Weed economic losses (national) — VERIFIED ✅ (03-Aug via Tavily MCP)
**Evidence:** Gharde et al. 2018, published in *Crop Protection* (ICAR-Directorate of Weed Research, Jabalpur): total actual economic loss of **~USD 11 billion/year** from weeds across **10 major crops** of India, based on **1,581 farm trials across 18 states**; worst-hit crops include groundnut (35.8% loss), soybean (31.4%). Weeds are the single **largest biotic loss factor at 33%** of crop losses — ahead of pathogens (26%), insects (20%), storage pests (7%), rodents (6%). ICAR-DWR: weed control costs ~₹6,000/ha in Kharif (≈33% of cost of cultivation) and ~₹4,000/ha in Rabi (≈22%); uncontrolled weeds can cause up to 65–70% yield loss at critical stages. Widely reported (Hindustan Times, AgriBusiness Global) and heavily cited (~810 citations).
**Sources:** [Gharde et al. 2018, CABI record](https://www.cabidigitallibrary.org/doi/full/10.5555/20183103206), [ISWS "Fifty Years of Weed Research in India" (PDF)](https://www.isws.org.in/Documents/Proceedings_of_conference/Conference-2018/Fifty_Years_of_Weed_Research_in_India.pdf), [Hindustan Times coverage](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/weeds-cause-annual-crop-loss-of-11-billion-in-india-says-study/story-stMbgxmiRPyJu9eNDwul2M_amp.html), [TAAS/ICAR-DWR strategy note (loss-factor breakdown)](https://taas.in/Upload/Programs/638519442915535952.pdf)
**Root cause:** weeds compete unseen for water/nutrients/light; impact "goes unnoticed" (lead author's words) until yield is already lost; control is labour- and herbicide-intensive.
**Verdict: PARTIAL.** The rover can DETECT and GPS-map weed patches and spot-spray herbicide via its mist nozzle — turning blanket herbicide into targeted application, same as the fungicide case. It CANNOT mechanically weed (no arm/implement) and cannot address the 33%-of-cost-of-cultivation labour weeding directly. Honest framing: weed *mapping + spot herbicide* is a real secondary use of the same vision+spray stack; mechanical weeding is out of scope for v1.

### P6. Grape export residue rejections — EU 2010 chlormequat incident — VERIFIED ✅ (03-Aug via Tavily MCP)
**Evidence:** In the **2010 season**, residue detections of the plant growth regulator **chlormequat chloride** caused the **rejection of multiple containers of Indian (Maharashtra) grapes** by the EU. Maharashtra accounts for ~95% of India's grape exports; EU-bound shipments had been ~37,000 tonnes in 2009 and did not recover to that level afterward. Times of India (via Fruitnet) quoted the Maharashtra Dept. of Agriculture's director of processing & planning: "The losses were so heavy that many farmers stayed away from export to the EU, despite it being the most lucrative market" — in 2011 most growers sold domestically or diverted to the Gulf to cut losses, and those who stayed faced higher residue-testing/compliance costs. A separate ToI report headlined the grape-export loss at **₹250 crore**.
**Sources:** [Fruitnet: "Indian grape exports to EU pick up" (2012, recounts the 2010 chlormequat rejections + grower quotes)](https://www.fruitnet.com/asiafruit/indian-grape-exports-to-eu-pick-up/13721.article), [Times of India: "Grape export suffers loss of Rs 250 crore"](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/pune/grape-export-suffers-loss-of-rs-250-crore/articleshow/6216912.cms)
**Root cause:** residues from calendar-based chemical use exceeded EU MRLs; no per-vine record of what was applied where/when → no defensible traceability at the consignment level; the EU's low MRLs + India's precautionary over-spraying (see P3) collide.
**Verdict: PARTIAL–SOLVABLE (traceability slice).** The rover cannot set MRL policy, but its GPS-stamped per-application spray LOG (what was sprayed, where, when, at what growth stage) is exactly the pre-harvest-interval / residue-window evidence an exporter needs. Combined with P3's shift from calendar to need-based spraying (fewer applications → lower residue load), this is a genuine export-compliance value-add — and it maps directly onto APEDA's existing GrapeNet traceability regime that emerged after 2010.

### Cotton pink bollworm (PBW) — VERIFIED ✅ (03-Aug via Tavily MCP) — extends the spot-spray story beyond grapes
**Evidence:** After Bt cotton (Bollgard-II, Cry1Ac+Cry2Ab) lost efficacy, PBW resistance emerged from 2014–15 and produced a major **2017–18 outbreak across ~11 million ha** in Maharashtra, Telangana, AP, Karnataka, Gujarat and MP; **field infestation ranged 8–92%**, and **~40% of seed cotton quality deteriorated** on top of direct yield loss. **Maharashtra (4.2 million ha, worst affected)** — a senior state agriculture official told the Economic Times that **~80% of the state's cotton area was affected** that season. Peer-reviewed yield-loss estimates cluster at **10–30%** (Fand et al. 2019, ScienceDirect; Springer J. Cotton Research; Nature Sci Reports 2025), with a more recent field-based estimate putting *actual* average PBW yield loss at ~3.75% but *potential* loss ~25%. Control now requires spraying **every 7–10 days** in the infested window, vs 2–3 sprays/acre for the whole crop before PBW returned.
**Sources:** [Fand et al. 2019 / PBW management & impact assessment (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0261219423002478), [Fand et al. 2025, field estimates of PBW yield loss (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0308521X24003962), [PBW resistance & mid-season pest establishment (PMC/Nature Sci Rep 2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11868544), [EIL dynamics for PBW (Springer J. Cotton Research)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42397-026-00258-0), [agriallis review (30% loss, spray-every-7–8-days figure)](https://agriallis.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/THE-RETURN-OF-PINK-BOLLWORM-IN-INDIA%E2%80%99S-BT-COTTON-FIELDS-AND-ITS-POSSIBLE-WAYS-TO-DEAL-WITH-IT.pdf)
**Root cause:** resistance broke the built-in Bt protection → PBW is back as a mid/late-season pest → farmers return to intensive calendar spraying (the exact behaviour that drove the Yavatmal poisonings, P1).
**Verdict: PARTIAL (detection + spot-spray + scouting).** The rover can't defeat resistance, but PBW is the *reason* cotton growers spray every 7–10 days and the *reason* people were in the field spraying at Yavatmal (P1). Early boll-damage/moth detection (pheromone-trap monitoring is already the state advisory) + targeted rather than blanket spraying + no human in the drift zone attacks the same pathway as the grape case, extending "scout + spot-spray" credibly to India's largest pesticide-intensive field crop. Honest limit: ground-camera imaging of bolls is harder than trellised grape bunches at rover height; scouting/trap-reading + spot-spray is the defensible v1 slice, not full boll inspection.

## EXPANDED PROBLEM LANDSCAPE — FULL SWEEP (03-Aug-2026, via Tavily MCP)

> The catalog above was grape/Maharashtra-wedge-focused. This section widens to the whole of Indian agriculture — big and small problems — each verified with a government or peer-reviewed number and judged honestly against the same rover constraints (sub-₹30k, Pi 5 + RGB camera, spot-spray mist nozzle, no arm, surface sensing only, Telegram UX). The recurring finding: the rover's ONE core capability — **computer-vision early detection + targeted spot-spray + row-by-row scouting/advisory** — generalizes across almost every *biotic* (pest/disease/weed) problem in India, while *abiotic/infrastructure/market/policy* problems remain honestly out of scope. That generalization is the "sky" the narrow grape pitch only hinted at.

### Category A — Biotic stress: pests, diseases, weeds (the rover's core "sky")

**P14. Fruit fly on mango/guava — VERIFIED ✅.** Oriental fruit fly (*Bactrocera dorsalis*) is a top-10 world pest and a direct pest of mango. Fruit-yield loss in mango ranges 1–31% (mean **16%**), and ICAR reports total fruit-fly losses of **27–42%, up to 90% in severe cases**. In rainy-season guava, infestation 20–46% with crop loss **16–40%** (Haseeb 2007, UP; Ukey et al. 2012: 13.4–40.6% by weight). Fruit flies are also a quarantine pest → export rejections.
**Sources:** [ICAR IndHort "Eco-friendly management of fruit fly in mango" (27–42%, up to 90%)](https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndHort/article/download/124866/47064/472878), [Plant Archives guava review (16–40%)](http://www.plantarchives.org/article/recent-advances-in-management-of-fruit-fly-for-guava-orchards-a-short-review.pdf), [Academia IPM Punjab study](https://www.academia.edu/71340498/)
**Verdict: PARTIAL.** Fruit flies are managed with pheromone/methyl-eugenol traps + timely cover sprays; the rover can carry trap-monitoring cameras and do targeted ground-level spot-spray, but mango fruit hangs in the overhead canopy (rover images trunk/low canopy only, same limit as P13). Guava on smaller/trained trees is a better fit. Detection + trap-reading + advisory is the defensible slice.

**P15. Fall armyworm on maize — VERIFIED ✅.** Invasive *Spodoptera frugiperda*, first seen in Karnataka May 2018, spread to 10+ states within 9 months. Reported Indian maize yield losses **33–36%** (initial reports), infestation 26–56% of plants; in severely affected areas losses run **20–80%** with some fields rendered unharvestable. Early estimate: ~**170,000 ha** of maize affected in the first wave (Karnataka 1.4 lakh ha, MP 85k ha, Rajasthan 59k ha). Polyphagous — also hits sugarcane, paddy, sorghum, even onion/banana.
**Sources:** [Frontiers Ecol & Evol (FAW invasion, 33–36% loss)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2023.1279640/full), [DownToEarth/PreventionWeb (170k ha, 10 states)](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/india-fall-armyworm-attack-damage-done), [Entomology Journal (20–50% loss)](https://www.entomologyjournals.com/assets/archives/2024/vol9issue11/9339.pdf)
**Verdict: SOLVABLE (strong).** FAW damage is visible on leaf whorls at exactly the plant height a ground rover images best, and control is need-based spot spraying of the whorl during early instars. Early CV detection → targeted spray before the larvae bore in is textbook precision IPM — one of the cleanest generalizations of the grape spot-spray stack to a staple field crop.

**P16. Rice stem borer & brown planthopper — VERIFIED ✅.** Yellow stem borer (*Scirpophaga incertulas*): national loss ~**20% (7.4 MMT)**, 27–34% in non-basmati, up to **80% in late-transplanted** crops; "dead heart"/"white ear" symptoms. Brown planthopper (*Nilaparvata lugens*): "hopperburn" can cause **10–80% loss** depending on stage, plus it vectors grassy/ragged stunt virus. Insect pests collectively cut average rice yield materially (stem borer 25%, BPH 15%, leaf folder 20% in one review).
**Sources:** [IRRI "Managing Rice Stem Borers" (1–80%)](http://www.knowledgebank.irri.org/images/docs/managing-rice-stem-borers-in-rice-wheat-systems.pdf), [PLOS One stem borer (10–40%)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0310234), [IRRI BPH book (hopperburn 10–80%)](http://books.irri.org/9711040220_content.pdf)
**Verdict: PARTIAL.** BPH hides at the base of the canopy (hard for a ground camera above the waterline) and stem borer larvae feed *inside* the stem (invisible until dead-heart appears). The rover can detect dead-heart/white-ear/hopperburn symptoms and map hotspots for targeted spray, but early internal detection is beyond RGB. Rice paddies (standing water) are also a hard mobility environment for a 4WD rover — honest mobility caveat.

**P17. Wheat rust & potato/tomato late blight — VERIFIED ✅.** Wheat stripe (yellow) rust causes epidemics roughly once a decade in NW India over ~10 M ha, up to **100% loss** in susceptible cultivars; cereal rusts cost ~**$5 billion/yr globally**. Late blight (*Phytophthora infestans*) — the Irish-famine pathogen — still inflicts ~**$6.7 billion/yr** globally in potato+tomato losses and control costs; it can destroy a field in days under cool wet conditions.
**Sources:** [ICAR-IIWBR stripe rust update (100% in susceptible)](https://iiwbr.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/RB-35-An-update-on-wheat-stripe-rust-management-in-India-A-success-story.pdf), [Bayer CropScience ($5B cereal rust)](https://www.cropscience.bayer.us/articles/cp/wheat-rust-diseases), [USDA-ARS late blight ($6.7B)](https://www.ars.usda.gov/news-events/news/research-news/2021/wild-potatoes-tapped-for-late-blight-guard-duty)
**Verdict: SOLVABLE (detection wedge, PARTIAL on spray).** Both diseases show diagnostic foliar symptoms (rust pustules, blight lesions) that RGB vision can flag early — and both are managed by *timely* fungicide the moment risk appears. A rover doing daily row scans gives the 24–48h early warning that decides whether a spray works or the field is lost. Spraying wheat/potato at scale needs more tank/boom than v1 carries (PARTIAL on the spray leg), but the early-detection-advisory value is real and large.

**Weed losses (P5, above) belong to this category too** — weeds are the single biggest biotic loss factor at 33% ($11B/yr, Gharde 2018), and weed-mapping + spot herbicide is the same RGB+nozzle stack. See P5.

### Category B — Soil, water, nutrients (mostly NOT the rover's job — honest tail, some advisory slices)

**P18. Groundwater depletion — VERIFIED ✅.** Punjab is India's worst case: of 150-odd assessment blocks, **~72–80% are over-exploited** (extraction > recharge); state groundwater development is **166%**; water table fell ~11 m from 1996–2018 in the paddy belt, dropping ~0.37–1 m/yr, and CGWB projects central Punjab aquifers could fall below 300 m by 2039 if trends hold. Nationally 839 of 5,723 blocks are over-exploited, concentrated in Punjab/Haryana/Rajasthan/Delhi (all extracting >100% of recharge). Root cause: free power + paddy in a semi-arid state + flood irrigation.
**Sources:** [NIPFP Punjab groundwater (80% over-exploited)](https://www.nipfp.org.in/media/documents/WP_2014_140.pdf), [MDPI Punjab water level (166% development, 11 m decline)](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/3/2426), [EACPM Addressing Groundwater Depletion (839/5723 blocks)](https://eacpm.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Addressing_Groundwater_Depletion_in_India.pdf)
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE.** Water table physics and crop-choice/power-subsidy policy are nowhere near a rover's reach. Listed because it's arguably India's single largest agri-sustainability threat — and being honest that we don't touch it is exactly the credibility Dr. Gajarushi wants. (Thin tangential slice: vision-based crop-water-stress flags could nudge irrigation timing, but that's a sensor/drip story, not ours.)

**P19. Soil health degradation & micronutrient deficiency — VERIFIED ✅.** The Soil Health Card programme (~52 million samples) found **39% of Indian soils zinc-deficient** and **64% deficient in organic carbon** (OC <0.5%); sulphur deficient in ~25%, boron ~45%(!) in newer analysis. AICRP-MSPE (>240k samples) put Zn deficiency higher (>50% on a 0.9 ppm threshold). S-deficiency spread from ~70 districts (early 1990s) to ~300 districts (2010s). India loses ~5.3 billion tonnes of topsoil/yr; ~30% of India's land is degraded and ~29% desertifying.
**Sources:** [Frontiers Soil Sci (39% Zn, 64% low OC)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/soil-science/articles/10.3389/fsoil.2024.1421652/full), [ICRIER soil health policy brief (SHC breakdown)](https://icrier.org/pdf/pb63_Improving-Soil-Health-Better-Crop-Health-and-Human-Nutrition.pdf), [IISS Soil Health Card (nutrient balance declining 12–14 MT/yr)](https://iiss.res.in/old/eMagazine/v4i1/12.pdf)
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE (core), thin advisory slice.** Real soil chemistry needs lab/probe testing (the SHC scheme itself); the rover does surface RGB sensing only, no deep NPK probing (explicit v1 constraint). It could log visible deficiency symptoms (interveinal chlorosis for Zn/Fe) as a *flag to get a soil test*, but honest answer: soil health is the Soil Health Card's job, not ours.

**P20. Fertilizer overuse & NPK imbalance — VERIFIED ✅.** India's NPK use ratio hit **8.2:3.2:1** (2012–13) vs the oft-cited 4:2:1 ideal — chronic nitrogen (urea) overuse and P/K underuse, driven by the subsidy structure (urea kept out of the Nutrient-Based Subsidy). Fertilizer subsidy bill ~**₹1.9 lakh crore** in 2025-26 (₹1.3 lakh crore on urea alone), yet response/efficiency keeps falling; N-use efficiency is only ~30–50%, P 15–20%, micronutrients 1–2%. Overuse contaminates groundwater (nitrate) and raises N₂O emissions.
**Sources:** [NITI Aayog/EPW "Fertiliser Use and Imbalance" (8.2:3.2:1)](https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-02/4_EPW_Article_Fertiliser_Use_and_Imbalance_in_India.pdf), [Springer Reg Env Change (subsidy-driven imbalance)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10113-025-02395-9), [DownToEarth CACP (bring urea under NBS)](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/agriculture/cacp-recommends-centre-to-bring-urea-under-nbs-regime-to-check-overuse-89907)
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE (policy), thin advisory slice.** The imbalance is a price-policy problem. A rover can't dose fertilizer without an arm/spreader and can't measure soil N in v1. Marginal slice: vision-based crop-vigor/deficiency mapping could feed variable-rate advice — but this is honestly an SHC + policy fix, not a rover wedge.

### Category C — Input quality, mechanization, storage, residue (mixed; a few real rover-adjacent slices)

**P21. Counterfeit/spurious agri-inputs — VERIFIED ✅.** Industry estimates put **~25% of India's pesticide market** as counterfeit/illegal (FICCI: up to 30% by volume, 25% by value); government 2023-24 testing found 2.7% of seed, 4.9% of fertiliser, 2.75% of pesticide samples substandard/spurious (an undercount given limited inspection). CropLife cited a Times of India report that **60% of Punjab's 2017 cotton crop was lost** to ineffective illegal pesticides. Fake inputs = wasted spend + crop failure + soil damage.
**Sources:** [ET Manufacturing (25% counterfeit, 2023-24 sample data)](https://manufacturing.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/chemicals/counterfeit-fertilizers-and-pesticides-indias-agricultural-crisis-and-chinas-role/123655763), [CropLife "Tackling India's Fake Pesticides" (60% Punjab cotton)](https://croplife.org/case-study/tackling-indias-fake-pesticides)
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE.** A rover can't authenticate a product in a bag. But there's an *indirect* value worth noting honestly: a rover that verifies "did the spray actually control the pest?" via before/after CV creates an efficacy record that surfaces a dud product faster than a farmer eyeballing it. Weak, but real.

**P22. Low farm mechanization & the custom-hiring model — VERIFIED ✅.** India's mechanization is **~40–47%**, far below US (95%), Brazil (75%), China (57–59.5%); farm power ~2.0–3.05 kW/ha vs a 4.0 kW/ha target. Small/marginal farmers (80%+ of holdings) can't afford individual machine ownership — which is precisely why the government pushes **Custom Hiring Centres (CHCs)** and FPO-based sharing, subsidizing 40% of CHC cost. Only ~5% of farmers accessed a bank loan for machinery.
**Sources:** [NAAS Policy Paper 135 (47% mechanization)](https://naas.org.in/Policy%20Papers/Policy%20135.pdf), [FICCI Agrimach (40–45%, CHC model)](https://ficci.in/public/storage/SPDocument/20682/agrimach.pdf), [PwC farm mechanisation (credit barrier)](https://www.pwc.in/assets/pdfs/consulting/management-consulting/farm-mechanisation-catalyst-sustainable-agricultural-growth.pdf)
**Verdict: CONTEXT (validates the business model, not a "problem we solve").** This is the P12 affordability argument's twin: the CHC/FPO shared-ownership channel is the *proven distribution model* for a sub-₹30k rover to reach 80% smallholders. It confirms *how* the rover gets to market, not a field problem it fixes.

**P23. Storage & cold-chain losses (beyond onion/PHL) — VERIFIED ✅.** Foodgrain storage losses ~**12–16 million tonnes/yr** (~₹50,000 cr), grains in traditional mud/bamboo structures losing ~6% (half to rodents); FCI CAP (open) storage exposes grain to rodents/moisture (godown loss up to 8% vs 0.2% in silos). For perishables, **~30–35% of fruits & vegetables (~40 MT, ~$13 billion)** are lost annually to cold-chain gaps; India has 8,815 cold stores (40.21 MT) but with skewed distribution and high operating cost (~$60/m³/yr vs <$30 West).
**Sources:** [ISHS cold-chain gap (30% F&V, $13B)](https://ishs.org/ishs-article/712_100), [IOSR agri storage infra (12–16 MT, ₹50k cr)](https://iosrjournals.org/iosr-jbm/papers/Vol17-issue5/Version-2/E017523743.pdf), [PIB 2172351 (8,815 cold stores, 40.21 MT)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2172351&reg=3&lang=2)
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE.** Storage structures and cold chain are capital-infrastructure problems (reaffirms the P4 onion verdict at national scale). Rover's only tangential tie-in is pre-storage CV grading (only sound produce into store) — the same thin slice already noted for onions.

**P24. Crop-residue / stubble burning — VERIFIED ✅.** India generates ~500–600+ MT crop residue/yr; ~140 MT is surplus and **~92 MT burned**, of which ~**39 MT is paddy straw** in Punjab/Haryana/UP/Rajasthan (Punjab alone burns ~19–20 MT, ~50,000+ fire events in bad years). Burning 1 tonne of paddy straw destroys ~5.5 kg N, 2.3 kg P, 25 kg K, 1.2 kg S plus soil microbes, and drives the annual Delhi-NCR air-pollution emergency (stubble contributes ~10–42% of Delhi winter PM2.5). Root cause: ~2-week turnaround between paddy harvest and wheat sowing + cheap disposal.
**Sources:** [SPRF "Defogging the Facts" (state residue/burn table)](https://sprf.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Stubble-Burning-in-North-India.-Defogging-the-Facts.pdf), [PMC stubble-burning 2021 (27 MT rice straw, 21 MT burnt in Punjab)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10275965), [Current Agri Research J (92 MT burned)](https://www.agriculturejournal.org/volume12number1/stubble-burning-in-india-environmental-concern-and-alternative-tools)
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE.** Residue management needs balers/Happy Seeders/mechanical implements and a market for straw — implements a no-arm rover can't carry. Pure honest "not us."

### Category D — Market, credit, knowledge, ecology (context + one genuine advisory adjacency)

**P25. Farmer debt, low income & distress — VERIFIED ✅.** NABARD: ~52% of agricultural households are indebted; ~30% still rely on non-institutional lenders (moneylenders). NSSO/SAS: average monthly farm-household income only **₹10,218** (2019). NCRB: **11,290 farming-sector suicides in 2022** (5,207 farmers + 6,083 labourers) — ~one per hour; ~400,000 cumulative 1995–2022; Maharashtra (4,248) + Karnataka (2,392) dominate, cotton belts worst.
**Sources:** [DownToEarth NCRB 2022 (11,290; ₹10,218 income)](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/agriculture/one-farmer-farm-labourer-dies-by-suicide-every-hour-in-india-ncrb-data-93184), [Wikipedia NCRB series 1995–2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmers%27_suicides_in_India), [J. Political Science (state policy, debt & suicides)](https://www.journalofpoliticalscience.com/uploads/archives/7-11-40-626.pdf)
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE (context — the human stakes).** No machine fixes debt or distress. But this is the emotional/why-it-matters backdrop of the whole pitch: the cotton belt with the most suicides (Maharashtra/Vidarbha) is the same Yavatmal belt of P1 and the pink-bollworm belt of the cotton entry — where cutting input waste and removing the human from the sprayer has the most human meaning. Frame as stakes, never as a claim to solve.

**P26. Pesticide residues in food (domestic safety) — VERIFIED ✅.** FSSAI tested 86,401 food samples (2022–25): **2.8% exceeded MRLs** nationally — but targeted state studies are far worse: a 2025 Haryana study found residues in **30.4% of vegetable samples** (with triazophos/chlorpyrifos health-risk indices >1), and older monitoring found 4–16% MRL exceedance in vegetables. This is the *domestic* mirror of the P6 EU-export residue problem: over-/calendar-spraying leaves residues on the food Indians eat.
**Sources:** [FSSAI via MedicalDialogues (2.8% > MRL, 86,401 samples)](https://health.medicaldialogues.in/health/fssai-finds-pesticide-residues-above-safe-limits-in-28-of-food-samples-153097), [ESPR Haryana vegetable study (30.4%)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ESPR...32.8358M/abstract), [PMC Gujarat F&V residue study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9547241)
**Verdict: PARTIAL (records and expert-reviewed decisions).** A complete product/active-ingredient/rate/date/PHI log can make compliance auditable, and threshold-based IPM can reduce unnecessary treatments. AgriRover has not yet proved that its surveillance removes a spray or lowers residues; any residue result requires a controlled crop trial and laboratory analysis.

**P27. Pollinator dependence & decline — VERIFIED ✅.** Animal pollination contributes an estimated economic value of **~₹1.1–4.9 lakh crore/yr** to Indian agriculture (Chandel/Rana estimate of insect-pollination economic value across ~108 entomophilous crops); crops with "great" dependence (mango, many fruits/veg) lose 40–90% yield without pollinators. Globally pollination is worth $235–577 billion/yr and pollinator-dependent crop volume has risen 300% in 50 years, even as bee populations decline (habitat loss + pesticides).
**Sources:** [ICAR "Economic benefits of animal pollination to Indian agriculture" (₹ lakh crore EV)](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/200b/193384d49e1bc94560d1face29a1fb07c79e.pdf), [Greenpeace Bees in Decline (global $ value)](https://www.greenpeace.org/static/planet4-international-stateless/2013/04/66f3eb6b-beesindecline.pdf)
**Verdict: NOT SOLVABLE directly; possible future co-benefit only.** A rover cannot pollinate. A validated reduction in insecticide use and exposure could later support a pollinator co-benefit, but AgriRover has no chemical-reduction or beneficial-insect field result today. Do not use this as a current ESG performance claim.

**P28. Extension / advisory reach gap — VERIFIED ✅.** Only **~6.8% of farmers** are reached by public extension (FAO 2012); one extension officer serves ~1,162 holdings (worker:farmer ratio below 1:5,000 vs the 1:750–1,100 guideline); India employs ~0.12 million extension staff vs China's 7.13 million; only ~40% of farmers access *any* modern-technology information, and of those the top source is "other farmers," not experts. Digital advisory (e.g., Odisha's Ama Krushi) shows 12:1–19:1 benefit-cost ratios where it reaches farmers.
**Sources:** [ICRISAT "Agriculture Extension System in India" (6.8% reach, 1:1162)](https://oar.icrisat.org/11401/1/Agriculture-Extension-System-in-India-A-Meta-analysis.pdf), [NAAS "Strengthening Agri-Extension" (0.12M vs China 7.13M)](https://naas.org.in/News/NN25032025.pdf), [VoxDev Ama Krushi (12:1–19:1 BCR)](https://voxdev.org/topic/agriculture/customised-agricultural-advice-scale-how-digital-extension-helps-indian-farmers)
**Verdict: PARTIAL / ENABLER, not an extension-agent replacement.** AgriRover can make selected field observations more repeatable, counted, geotagged and auditable. A KVK/qualified agronomist and systems such as NPSS/HORTSAP remain responsible for interpretation and label-compliant advice. The farmer interface should deliver a short local-language `ACT`, `WATCH` or `NO TREATMENT` message plus evidence, never an autonomous dosage.

### Generalization thesis: one platform, bounded problem class

AgriRover is **not a general-purpose answer to Indian agriculture**. It is a reusable field-intelligence and targeted-intervention platform for problems that are visible early, reachable from crop-row height, and improved by fast local action.

1. **Strongest fit — selected, observable biotic stress.** The reusable loop is: sample or scan an approved observation → count and geotag it → qualified expert applies the crop protocol → record the action → revisit and verify. Each crop still needs its own camera geometry, sampling unit, model and field validation.
2. **Partial fit — visibility, access or scale breaks one leg.** Concealed pests, internal stem damage, overhead fruit, flooded paddies and broad-acre spray volumes still benefit from mapping/advice, but exceed one or more v1 hardware constraints.
3. **No fit — the root cause lives outside the field robot.** Groundwater policy, soil chemistry, fertilizer subsidies, storage, cold chain, counterfeit inputs, debt, market structure and residue handling require infrastructure, institutions, laboratory measurement or heavy machinery. Calling these out protects the credibility of the solvable claims.
4. **Product implication — launch crop-by-crop, keep the evidence loop reusable.** Launch tomato first in Junnar. Chilli follows only after macro leaf/flower or trap counting agrees with expert counts. Grapes remain a later scouting/traceability offer because fungal canopy treatment and ~1,000 L/ha normal spray volume do not fit v1. The horizontal opportunity is "sample + count + review + document + verify," not "a robot that solves every farm problem."

### Commercial interpretation after the adoption research

1. **Start with tomato protection surveillance in Junnar.** Chilli is second only after macro leaf/flower or trap counting hardware passes expert count agreement; the two crops must not share unsupported model claims.
2. **Sell a decision workflow, not a robot specification.** Scan → count/geotag → threshold-based, agronomist-reviewed advice → treatment record → outcome verification.
3. **Keep grapes as a later traceability/scouting product.** APEDA's plot, spray and PHI records are a real workflow, but v1 cannot replace normal high-volume canopy application.
4. **Use FPO/KVK distribution.** Shared seasonal service removes the farmer ownership barrier and adds the trust layer AI advice needs.
5. **Label every number.** Problem statistics and external IPM/spot-spray results validate the opportunity only; AgriRover must measure its own accuracy, throughput, savings, yield safety and paid renewal.
6. **Retain the honesty column.** Storage, market prices, wildlife and tree-fruit harvesting remain outside the product's defensible scope.
